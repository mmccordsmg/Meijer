FAQs:

      Q: This looks like it just creates accounts and clips coupons, what makes it so dangerous?
      A: Literally nothing.
      
      Q: Did you just write the software, or did you use it as well?
      A: I have never been in a Meijer store, or used this software outside of testing and debugging.

      Q: There must be more to the story.  Why criminal charges?  
      A: There was a glitch in Meijer's server-side coupon handler that would add random* rewards to an account when certain coupon numbers were clipped.  I never figured out the pattern, but by clipping thousands of coupon codes, rewards were activated as if they had been earned.  No escalation of access occurred or was alleged.  Two users of the software used this glitch to obtain approximately $72k in merchandise from various stores.  They were convicted (via guilty pleas) of 1 count each of felony conspiracy to commit wire fraud.

      Q: If Meijer's server says the coupon/reward is legitimate, how can redeeming it be criminal?
      A: You got me.

      Q: What's the difference between a coupon and a reward?
      A: Meijer doesn't seem to know the answer to this, so I doubt I can explain it.

      Q: So how did you get wrapped up in it?  Isn't authoring software protected speech under the First Amendment?
      A: A lot to unravel here.  First, I was in active communication with these users, adding features at their request.  This potentially opened me up to being a member of the 'conspiracy'.  Once they opted not to defend their cases, I had very few options.  I likely had a decent First Amendment argument, but, in my research, many juries fail to reach the correct conclusion and an appeal or two may be required.  I have a family and kids to support.  I can't risk sitting in prison waiting for an appeal. Also, taking a guilty plea cost me $50,000 in legal expenses.  A trial would have run $100,000, plus more if we had to appeal the ruling.

      Q: Speaking of prison, what were you sentenced to?
      A: 8 months of home confinement, 2 years of probation, a $5,000 fine, and joint and several restitution of the full $72k.

      Q: Home confinement for writing software?  How does that make sense?
      A: 🤷‍♂️ I guess the public needs to be protected from me.

      Q: You never asked for any money or stepped foot in a store.  Why do you owe restitution?
      A: I guess it's the law?  Luckily for me, the other two guys already paid the full amount, so I'm not actually on the hook for that.
